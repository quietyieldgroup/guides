Capability Statement for Landscaping Businesses: 2026 Guide

Current as of 19 November 2026. General information, not advice: check licence requirements with the QBCC (in Queensland) or your state's licensing body, and insurance with your insurer or broker. Each buyer sets its own requirements, and its quote or tender documents always come first. Licence examples below are Queensland's; other states differ.

Parks, streetscapes, school grounds and estate entries are bought by councils, developers and head contractors, and they may ask for a capability statement. Queensland's state development department calls it "like a resume for your business". Here are the questions landscaping firms ask about writing one.

Who will ask for it?

BuyerWhy it matters
CouncilsMuch council work is not publicly tendered. In Queensland, the default rules have councils invite written quotes from at least 3 suppliers for medium-sized contracts, and the council chooses who to ask. A one-page statement with a short introduction helps its buyers know you exist.
Tenders and panelsBusiness Queensland lists what tenders must include, among them "a company profile and capability statement", "whether you propose to subcontract" and "applicable insurances" (tendering tips).
Head contractorsQueensland's department says of major project supply chains: "Most businesses fit into Tiers 2 and 3, where local suppliers are vital for efficiency, maintenance, and building regional capability."

What licences go in?

For each: type, number, issuing body and expiry, plus the supplier registrations and panels you are on. Which licences you need depends on your state and the work, so check with the national ABLIS tool, which helps you "find out which licences you need to start, run and grow your business".

In Queensland, the QBCC says you must hold a licence to carry out building work "valued over $3,300", and work "of any value" involving drainage, plumbing and drainage, gas fitting and other listed classes (QBCC: when you need a licence). If any of your work is building work, check with the QBCC which class covers it. QBuild, the state's building agency, runs a contractor register: it asks for evidence of public liability insurance, and says that if you're self-employed you must have personal injury insurance.

What insurance details do buyers want?

Type, insurer, sum insured and expiry for each policy. "Fully insured" with no figures tells a buyer nothing.

  • Public liability "covers you if someone dies, gets injured or has their property damaged" (business.gov.au). Buyers set minimums: Local Buy, owned by the Local Government Association of Queensland, sets a minimum of $20 million public liability for all its suppliers.
  • Workers' compensation: WorkCover Queensland says "If you employ workers in Queensland, you must have workers' compensation insurance."
  • Professional indemnity "covers mistakes, neglect or breaches of contract that result in a loss for your client". If you don't hold it, say so. Our kit's landscape and civil example (a made-up firm) says designs come from the client's engineer, so it lists PI as "Not held".

Keep certificates of currency ready: Business Queensland says a customer "may request a certificate of currency", that this "is typical in a business-to-business sales scenario, such as a tender", and that one "can usually be provided to you immediately via email".

How do we show our plant?

Queensland's department says to "Show your track record, key staff, awards, specialised equipment/capabilities and management systems". List the items that prove you can do the job, with number, size and capacity. The kit's example lists 3 excavators with their sizes: "1 x 1.7 t, 2 x 5 t, with tilt buckets".

What about safety on site?

If you do high risk construction work, say you use safe work method statements. In Queensland a SWMS must be prepared before the work starts and, on a construction project, a copy given to the principal contractor (WorkSafe Qld: SWMS). Add one safety fact you can prove and how you check quality on larger jobs.

How do we show past projects?

Western Australia's Small Business Development Corporation says: "List any significant projects or achievements (including dollar value)", and suggests grouping experience "by location or service" (WA SBDC). For each of your three closest jobs: client, location, value, completion date, what you did, and a result the client would confirm, such as the kit example's "Accepted by council at on-maintenance inspection with no defects." Match a referee to each project, and ask them first.

Can we claim regional or local?

Only in the buyer's terms. The Queensland Procurement Policy 2026 defines a regional business as a Queensland supplier with its main business address on the ABR outside south-east Queensland, a small business as one "employing less than 20 people", and a local workforce as workers living "within a 250 km radius" of your place of business who deliver the work. The kit's example firm has its depot in south-east Queensland, so it says plainly that it does not claim regional status. In state contracts, specific commitments you make in an offer are written into the contract.
