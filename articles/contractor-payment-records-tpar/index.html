What Records Do I Need for a TPAR? Contractor Payments

Current as of 19 November 2026. General information only, not tax advice. ATO pages change, so check the linked pages, or ask a registered tax or BAS agent, before you lodge.

For a Taxable payments annual report (TPAR) you need two sets of records: each contractor's details (ABN, name and address), and a record of each payment to them, with the date, invoice, GST and total paid. At year end you total each contractor's payments and report those. Here is what the ATO asks you to keep.

For each contractor: who they are

The ATO's contractor details page says your TPAR must include, for each contractor you pay:

  • their Australian business number (ABN), if known. If a contractor's ABN changed during the year, include each ABN
  • their name (business name or individual's name)
  • their address.

The ATO adds that it "may ask for extra information about your contractors", including their phone number, email address and bank account details if you pay them by electronic bank transfer. Keep those on file too.

Check the ABN as invoices arrive

The ATO's easier-to-read guidance (written for cleaning businesses) sets out a routine:

  1. When you get the first invoice from a new contractor, use ABN Lookup to make sure the information is right.
  2. Each time you get a new invoice, check the ABN is the same as in your records.
  3. If it isn't, look up the new ABN. If it doesn't match the contractor, phone them to see if they made a mistake.

ABN Lookup or the ATO app checks the contractor's ABN, name and GST registration. If the ABN on an invoice differs from your record, the ATO says to create a new contractor record if necessary.

For each payment: what you paid

The same ATO guidance says to record, for each payment you make to a contractor:

  • the date
  • the invoice number
  • details of the service or materials they provided
  • any tax you held back because they didn't give you their ABN
  • the GST amount paid to them
  • the total amount paid to them, including GST.

The ATO's own Taxable payments reporting worksheet uses the same layout. Its header asks for the financial year and the contractor's name, ABN, address and contact number; its columns are date paid, details (for example invoice number, labour and materials), total tax withheld where ABN not quoted, GST amount paid, and total amount paid including GST. "This worksheet is for your records only – do not send it to the ATO."

At year end: the totals you report

For each contractor, the TPAR reports the year's totals "in whole dollars only (excluding cents)" of:

TotalFrom your payment records
Gross amount paidIncluding GST and any tax withheld
Total GST you paid themThe GST column
Total tax withheld where an ABN was not quotedThe withholding column

Before you lodge, the ATO's lodgment page also says to check whether your business software can prepare and lodge the TPAR.

What not to record as a TPAR payment

From the ATO's payments to report page:

  • Labour and materials on one invoice: record and report the total payment amount. Payments for materials only are left out.
  • Cash basis: only report payments you made on or before 30 June. An invoice still unpaid on 30 June stays out of that year.
  • Also left out: incidental labour, labour-hire or on-hire workers, payments to employees, payments within a consolidated group, and payments for private and domestic projects.
  • Only payments for the relevant services count. For example, a building business only reports payments to contractors for building and construction services.

Contractors who don't quote an ABN

"Payers must withhold 47% (from 1 July 2017) from the total payment for a supply" unless an exception applies, such as a payment of $75 or less excluding GST (ATO). Record what you withheld. You report it in either the TPAR or the PAYG payment summary – withholding where ABN not quoted (NAT 3448), "not both". The ATO's pages differ on whether a no-ABN contractor's payments go in the TPAR at all, so ask your agent.

Why good records matter

The ATO says TPAR amounts "will now be pre-filled" in your contractors' tax returns, and it uses TPAR data to check for red flags such as not including income. You don't need to give contractors the details you report. The 2026-27 TPAR covers payments from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027 and "must be lodged by 28 August each year".
