How Much to Charge for End of Lease Cleaning in Australia

Current as of 17 November 2026. General information only, not legal, tax, financial or employment advice. Award rates usually change every 1 July, so check the current Fair Work Ombudsman pay guide before you rely on any rate.

There is no official price for an end of lease clean, and this article doesn't quote what other cleaners charge. What you can work out is a price that covers your own costs. Below is the method, a worked example, the factors that change an end of lease quote, and what the job covers.

How to work out an end of lease cleaning price

Build the price from cost, in this order:

  1. Time the job. Estimate labour hours on site from the room count or from similar jobs. Minutes-per-room figures are only a starting guess: time a few of your own jobs and replace them.
  2. Work out your true cost per labour hour. For employees that is the award rate, plus a leave on-cost for full-time and part-time staff, plus super. If you clean yourself, use the hourly amount you want to earn, and remember nobody pays your super or leave, so build them in.
  3. Add the job's direct costs: travel time, supplies, and vehicle cost per kilometre.
  4. Add a share of overheads: monthly insurance, software and other fixed costs, plus your admin hours for quoting, invoicing and marketing, divided by jobs per month.
  5. Add your margin. Divide cost by (1 − margin). A 15% margin means $15 profit in every $100 of price (ex GST). Adding 15% to cost instead, a mark-up, gives only about a 13% margin.
  6. Add GST if you are registered. GST is 10%, and one-eleventh of what the customer pays is GST you pass on (business.gov.au).

The labour cost input

Under the Cleaning Services Award 2020 [MA000022], a Level 1 cleaner's hourly rate from the first full pay period starting on or after 1 July 2026 depends on the day (FWO pay guide, published 24 June 2026):

The rate is only the start. Super guarantee is 12% for 2026-27 and, under Payday Super, must be paid for each payday from 1 July 2026 (ATO). Full-time and part-time staff also get 4 weeks' annual leave (Fair Work) and 10 days' personal leave, pro-rata for part-time (Fair Work). Allowing for leave and super, the guide puts a Level 1 cleaner on weekday daytime hours at $34.75 an hour worked full-time and $37.91 casual, before public holidays and workers' compensation.

In this example every extra hour on site adds another $39.43 of cost before margin. That matters for end of lease work: price the job on the hours you have timed, not on the example's 3 hours.

End of lease factors that change the price

FactorWhat to allow for
Minimum shiftsPart-time and casual cleaners must be paid at least 2 hours at a site of up to 2,000 m², even if the job takes less (award clause 13.5). A 1-hour minimum applies only in a narrow case for a lone cleaner at a small stand-alone site.
Weekend workFor full-time staff, Saturday is 150% of the minimum hourly rate, Sunday 200% and public holidays 250%. Part-time and casual percentages are higher.
TravelTime travelling between workplaces is paid "as if they were working" (clause 17.12).
ExtrasAgree in writing what's included and what isn't, for example carpet steam cleaning, pest treatment, or outside windows above ground floor.
Re-cleansAgree how re-clean requests will be handled (time limit, what counts) before you start, and how agent or landlord lists are handled.
Deposit and paymentDecide whether you take a deposit for end of lease jobs, and when payment is due.
GSTIf registered, a $100 GST-inclusive price leaves you $90.91. If you aren't registered you don't include GST, and invoices must show that GST was not included (business.gov.au). You must register once your GST turnover reaches $75,000, within 21 days (ATO).

End of lease cleaning checklist: what the job covers

Before quoting, ask the client for the entry condition report and any list from the agent or landlord: it tells you what standard is expected. A checklist is a work list, not a promise about bond outcomes. The kit's end of lease checklist is grouped into five areas. A selection from each:

AreaExamples from the checklist
ThroughoutCobwebs on ceilings, cornices and corners; light fittings and fans; skirting boards; walls spot-cleaned; windows inside, sills and tracks; built-in wardrobes inside
KitchenOven inside (racks, trays, glass, seals); rangehood and filters degreased; cupboards and drawers inside and out; dishwasher filter and seals
Bathrooms, toilets and laundryShower screen soap scum and water marks; tiles and grout scrubbed; toilet inside, outside, base and cistern; exhaust fans
Outside (if agreed)Balcony or patio swept and mopped; ground-level outdoor windows; bins emptied and rinsed
Hand-overDated photos of each room; walk-through or check against the condition report; keys returned and recorded

Use the same list to time your jobs. Each item you tick is labour hours, and labour hours drive the price.
