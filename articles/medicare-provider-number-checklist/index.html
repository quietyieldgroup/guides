Medicare Provider Number for Allied Health: 2026 Checklist

Current as of 17 November 2026. General information only, not legal or financial advice. Rules, forms and processing times change, so check each linked official page before you apply.

Opening a physio, psychology, OT, podiatry, speech pathology, dietetics, chiro or osteo practice? The Medicare provider number unlocks most other registrations. This checklist covers the order, what to have ready, the HPOS steps and the traps that hold applications up.

The order: what has to happen first

Most registrations depend on the one before, so the order matters:

  1. Professional registration. Your Ahpra National Board, or your professional body if your profession has no National Board (speech pathology and dietetics are not in Ahpra's list of Boards).
  2. ABN, plus a business name and GST registration if they apply to you.
  3. PRODA, your individual government login.
  4. HPOS, linked inside PRODA. This is where you apply for and manage provider numbers.
  5. Medicare provider number, one for each location.

Are you eligible?

Services Australia says that to claim Medicare benefits, allied health professionals must:

  • meet the qualification requirements in the Health Insurance (Allied Health Services) Determination 2014;
  • be registered with Ahpra or a respective board or organisation;
  • be recognised as an allied health professional in their field by Services Australia;
  • be in private practice, working in a private capacity.

You also need a provider number for each practice location, and you can't claim a Medicare benefit until Services Australia has issued your number for that location. You are not eligible if your membership or registration has been suspended or has expired, or your membership level has changed.

Step 1: Register your PRODA account

PRODA (Provider Digital Access) is the government's identity check and login for providers. Services Australia's PRODA registration page sets out the steps. Have ready:

  • A personal email address. Services Australia recommends you don't use a group or work email. Your activation code and login codes go to it.
  • Either a Digital ID, or three Australian identity documents from the list PRODA shows you. Each document can be used once.
  • Three security questions and answers.

Once registered, you get an email confirming your username and your Registration Authority (RA) number. Write the RA number down: you need it to be added to an organisation, and anyone you later make your HPOS delegate needs theirs. If you registered with documents, verify your identity with them before you try to use any services.

You can only register one PRODA account in your name, and you can't create one for someone else: each staff member registers their own. Your account doesn't expire, so it comes with you when you leave a clinic.

Step 2: Link HPOS

From My Linked Services in PRODA, link HPOS (Health Professional Online Services). Services Australia says that if you don't have a healthcare identifier you can still link HPOS for basic functions, including applying for a Medicare provider number.

Switch on mail alerts straight away: in HPOS, select Messages, then My mailbox, then Settings. The outcome of an online provider number application arrives as an HPOS message, so you don't want it sitting unread.

Step 3: Apply for the provider number

Your first (initial) number

An initial number is your first one, or the one you need when you start working in a new profession. Per Services Australia's initial provider number page:

  1. Log in to PRODA and go to HPOS.
  2. Select My Details, then My Digital Provider Number Registration, then Apply.
  3. Complete the questions and submit. You can save and come back.

An additional number (new location)

You need an additional number when you work at a new practice location, or provide a new service at the same location. Per the additional provider number page:

  1. Log in to PRODA and go to HPOS.
  2. Select My details, then My provider numbers.
  3. Select Create a new provider location, read the privacy note, answer the questions and submit.

Ahpra-registered allied health professionals with general or limited registration, and non-Ahpra registered allied health professionals, can apply online. If you are not registered with Ahpra (for example a speech pathologist or dietitian), you must be registered with a respective board or organisation.

The paper route: form HW093

If you can't apply online, use form HW093, which covers initial and additional numbers. Complete every section and send all supporting documents: incomplete paper applications are returned by mail and you start again from the beginning.

Three address traps

These come from Services Australia's provider number procedures:

  • The 24-character limit. If your locality (suburb or town) is more than 24 characters including spaces, HPOS applications show an error. Count before you start, and use the spelling Australia Post uses. If you hit the error, call the Medicare provider enquiry line.
  • Home addresses can't be hidden. Services Australia can't suppress a provider number address, so a home address used as a practice location may be publicly available. There is a narrow exception for telehealth-only or mobile practitioners whose home is their only location.
  • You can't just edit the address later. Typing over an existing location's address is only allowed in narrow cases, such as a keying error or a move to another suite in the same building. A real move is a new location and a new number.

Rules that catch people

  • You can't use anyone else's provider number, including a colleague's or a previous owner's. Buying a practice doesn't transfer the seller's numbers.
  • If a service has no Medicare benefit, you must tell the patient before you treat them. Services Australia says it is an offence not to.

What the number sets off

DVA says most providers are registered with it automatically when they receive their Medicare provider number, although some provider types must register directly, and DVA's form D9537 (ABN and GST status) is a separate step. Several health funds also take your details from Medicare, while others need their own form. HICAPS terminal claiming is set up separately again.
