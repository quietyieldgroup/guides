TPAR for Builders: Reporting Payments to Subcontractors

Current as of 19 November 2026. General information only, not tax advice. ATO pages change, so check the linked pages, or ask a registered tax or BAS agent, before you lodge.

If your building business pays subbies, you may have to lodge a Taxable payments annual report (TPAR) each year, and the test for builders is different from the one the other TPAR industries use. Here are the questions builders ask, answered from the ATO's own pages.

Do I need to lodge a TPAR?

The 10% test other industries use doesn't apply to building and construction. Instead, the ATO says that if you are running a business primarily in the building and construction industry, you must lodge if you make payments to contractors or subcontractors for building and construction services and you have an ABN (ATO: Building and construction services).

You count as "primarily" in building and construction if any one of these applies:

  • in the current financial year, 50% or more of your business income is earned from building and construction services
  • in the current financial year, 50% or more of your business activity relates to building and construction services
  • in the financial year immediately before, 50% or more of your business income was earned from building and construction services.

For 2026-27, the year before is 2025-26. If you paid no contractors at all, the ATO says you should lodge a TPAR Non-lodgment advice instead.

Which subbie payments go in?

Only payments to contractors for building and construction services. The ATO's examples include bricklaying; concreting, including formwork, pouring and finishing; electrical work; landscaping construction, including paving and design; painting; plumbing work; roofing and guttering; and waterproofing. What do I report for each subbie?

The ATO lists the details (ATO: TPAR contractor details):

  • ABN, if known, and each ABN if it changed during the year
  • name and address
  • the gross amount paid for the year, including GST and any tax withheld
  • the total GST you paid them, and the total tax withheld where an ABN was not quoted.

Totals go in whole dollars only, excluding cents. Report on a cash basis: only payments you made on or before 30 June, so a subbie's invoice still unpaid on 30 June stays out of that year's report.

The subbie with no ABN

If a supplier doesn't quote an ABN, payers must withhold 47% from the total payment unless an exception applies, such as a payment of $75 or less excluding GST. If you don't withhold, you may have to pay a penalty equal to the amount you should have withheld (ATO: Statement by a supplier not quoting an ABN). The ATO's payments to report page says to report the amount withheld in either the TPAR or the NAT 3448 form, not both.

What happens if I lodge late?

In one ATO case study, a construction business failed to lodge by the 28 August 2024 deadline. The ATO issued the business a failure to lodge penalty of $1,650 and required it to submit the overdue TPAR with accurate contractor payment details. The failure to lodge penalty is one penalty unit for every 28 days (or part of 28 days) overdue, up to 5 units, multiplied by 2 for medium withholders and 5 for large ones.

Your subbies see the figures too: the ATO says TPAR amounts "will now be pre-filled" in contractors' tax returns.

When is the 2026-27 TPAR due?

By 28 August each year. For payments from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027, that is 28 August 2027, a Saturday. The ATO's general weekend rule would make it Monday 30 August by our count, but the TPAR pages just say 28 August, so lodge by Friday 27 August 2027 to be safe. Paper TPARs are no longer accepted.
