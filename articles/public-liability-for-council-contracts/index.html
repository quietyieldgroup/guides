Public Liability Insurance for Council Contracts in Queensland

Current as of 18 November 2026. General information only, not legal or insurance advice. Each buyer sets its own insurance requirements, and its quote or tender documents always come first. Talk to your broker or insurer about your own cover.

Ask how much public liability you need for council or government work in Queensland and the honest answer is that there is no single figure. Buyers set it job by job, in the request for quote or tender. What you can know in advance are the published minimums small trade and service firms run into most, when buyers ask for proof, and what to have ready so an insurance question never costs you a job.

What public liability insurance covers

business.gov.au puts it simply: "Public liability insurance covers you if someone dies, gets injured or has their property damaged". It adds that "You may need product liability insurance if you make, sell or supply goods" (business.gov.au: types of business insurance). Some requirements are written as the two together, as public and products liability.

These are the figures and requirements the Queensland buyers and licences below publish. Where no figure is shown, the buyer sets it in its own documents.

Why the $20 million figure matters for council work

Under the Local Government Regulation 2012's default rules, councils can skip quotes and tenders when they buy through a Local Buy arrangement, an approved contractor list, a register of pre-qualified suppliers or a preferred supplier arrangement. Local Buy, owned by the Local Government Association of Queensland, says 71 Queensland councils use its platform. So if you want to be on its arrangements, $20 million public liability is the floor.

You join Local Buy by responding to its tenders, which it opens on 1 February, 1 May, 1 August and 1 November each year. They are generally open for the whole month, so the round that opened on 1 November 2026 may still be open; check its open tenders page. After that, the next round opens 1 February 2027.

If your cover is below what the buyer asks

Treat it as a gate, not a detail. The bid/no-bid sheet in our paid guide asks first: "We hold, or can get before closing, the insurance levels asked for." If the answer is no, don't bid. If the documents don't say what limit they want, ask before closing: Business Queensland's advice is "If you have questions, contact the tender coordinator". If you need a higher limit, talk to your broker well before the closing date.

The other insurances buyers ask about

  • Workers' compensation. "If you employ workers in Queensland, you must have workers' compensation insurance" (WorkCover Queensland). QBuild notes: "You don't need to provide WorkCover insurance if you don't have any employees."
  • Personal injury insurance if you are self-employed. QBuild says "you must have personal injury insurance for your own protection".
  • Professional indemnity, if you do design or give advice, or a buyer asks for it. business.gov.au says it "covers mistakes, neglect or breaches of contract that result in a loss for your client". If you don't hold it, write "not held" rather than leaving a gap.
  • Your subcontractors' cover. If you will subcontract, tenders ask "whether you propose to subcontract". Keep each regular subcontractor's licences and insurances on file.

Certificates of currency: have them before you're asked

Business Queensland says a customer "may request a certificate of currency to prove that you have the required insurance cover. This is typical in a business-to-business sales scenario, such as a tender." It adds that this "is a standard request that can usually be provided to you immediately via email" (Business Queensland: insurance).

Even so, don't leave it until the request arrives. Invited quotes can come with short deadlines, and a state agency may ask only once you're shortlisted, when you least want a delay. Keep current certificates as PDFs in one folder with your ABN record, licences and capability statement. Renew them before they expire, and re-send the new ones to any panel you're on.

Show the number in your capability statement

Tenders commonly ask for "a company profile and capability statement" alongside "applicable insurances", so put your cover in it. List each policy with its type, insurer, sum insured and expiry: "Public and products liability $20 million" tells a buyer something, "fully insured" doesn't. Insurance checklist for council and government work

  1. Find your current public liability limit and expiry date on your policy schedule.
  2. Check the minimums for where you want to work: $20 million for Local Buy; at least $5 million public and products liability if you're a Queensland electrical contractor; whatever each request for quote or tender states.
  3. Ask your broker for certificates of currency for public liability and, if you employ workers, workers' compensation.
  4. Add personal injury cover if you're self-employed and want QBuild work, and professional indemnity if you design, advise or a buyer asks for it.
  5. File the certificates with your other tender documents, and diarise each expiry date.
  6. Put the limits, insurers and expiry dates in your capability statement and supplier profiles.
  7. Before every bid, read the insurance clause in the documents. If you can't meet it by closing, don't bid.
