TPAR for Cleaning Businesses: Paying Contract Cleaners

Current as of 19 November 2026. General information only, not tax advice. ATO pages change, so check the linked pages, or ask a registered tax or BAS agent, before you lodge.

If your cleaning business sends contractors or subcontractors to clean for your customers, the ATO probably wants a Taxable payments annual report (TPAR) from you each year. Here are the questions cleaning business owners ask, answered from the ATO's own pages.

Do I need to lodge a TPAR for my contract cleaners?

The ATO says you must lodge if all three of these apply (ATO: Cleaning services):

  1. your business provides cleaning services to other people or businesses
  2. you paid contractors or subcontractors to provide these cleaning services on behalf of your business
  3. your business has an ABN.

Payments to your employees don't go in a TPAR. If you paid no contractors at all in the year, the ATO says you should lodge a TPAR Non-lodgment advice instead.

Cleaning is only part of the business? Use the 10% test

Add up what your business received for cleaning in the financial year, including cleaning done by your employees as well as by contractors, and divide it by your business income. At 10% or more of your gross business income, you lodge. Under 10%, you don't have to, but the ATO says you can choose to lodge if you are under the threshold or unsure whether you will be.

The ATO's own example is a building maintenance business that received $10,000 for office cleaning out of $100,000 of income. That is 10%, so it must lodge. In the TPAR it reports the $5,000 it paid its contractors for cleaning, and doesn't include the $6,000 it paid its employees.

What counts as cleaning?

The ATO's list of cleaning activities: carpet, chimney, exterior (except sandblasting), gutter, interior, park and park facilities, road and street sweeping, and swimming pool cleaning. Also left out: if the business pays a cleaner for private purposes, such as cleaning your own home, that payment isn't reported.

My contractors supply their own chemicals. What do I report?

The whole invoice. The ATO says to "Report the total payment amount if an invoice you receive from a contractor includes both labour and materials." Its cleaning example is exactly this case: a cleaning company whose contractor's invoices included the contractor's own cleaning products must include the full $39,600 it paid. Payments for materials only are left out (ATO: Payments to report).

What should I record for each contract cleaner?

The ATO's easier-to-read page for cleaning businesses says to record, for each payment:

  • the date and the invoice number
  • details of the service or materials they provided
  • any tax you held back because they didn't give you their ABN
  • the GST amount paid to them
  • the total amount paid to them, including GST.

Check the ABN when the first invoice arrives, using ABN Lookup. On every later invoice, check the ABN is the same as in your records. If it has changed, look the new one up, and if it doesn't match the contractor, phone them.

Three rules that catch cleaning businesses

  • No ABN quoted: payers must withhold 47% unless an exception applies, such as a payment of $75 or less excluding GST. If you don't withhold, you may have to pay a penalty equal to the amount you should have withheld (ATO: Statement by a supplier not quoting an ABN).
  • Cash basis: only report payments you made on or before 30 June. An invoice still unpaid on 30 June stays out of that year's report.
  • Whole dollars: totals for each contractor go in whole dollars only, excluding cents.

Your figures reach your cleaners: TPAR amounts "will now be pre-filled" in contractors' tax returns (ATO).

When is the 2026-27 TPAR due?

"A TPAR must be lodged by 28 August each year" (ATO: TPAR). The report for payments from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027 is due 28 August 2027, which is a Saturday. Under the ATO's general rule for weekend due dates, that would move to Monday 30 August by our count, but the TPAR pages just say 28 August, so the safe choice is to lodge by Friday 27 August 2027.

Lodge online: through SBR-enabled business software, Online services for business, Online services for individuals and sole traders, or your registered tax or BAS agent. Paper TPARs are no longer accepted. Late reports attract the ATO's failure to lodge penalty: one penalty unit for every 28 days (or part of 28 days) overdue, up to 5 units.
