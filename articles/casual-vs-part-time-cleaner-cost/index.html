Casual vs Part-Time Cleaner: Which Costs More? (2026 Award)

Current as of 19 November 2026. General information only, not legal, tax, financial or employment advice. Award rates usually change every 1 July, so check the current Fair Work Ombudsman pay guide before you rely on any rate.

Short answer: under the Cleaning Services Award, a casual cleaner has the higher hourly rate, but a part-time cleaner can cost you more for each hour they actually clean. That is because part-time staff get a 15% allowance on every ordinary hour and paid leave as well. Here are the numbers for 2026-27 and the rules behind them.

The loading and the allowance

The Cleaning Services Award 2020 [MA000022] adds two different amounts to the minimum hourly rate:

  • Casuals: "a loading of 25% in addition to the minimum hourly rate" (clause 11.2).
  • Part-time: "for each ordinary hour worked an allowance of 15% in addition to the minimum hourly rate" (clause 10.2).

The on-costs that close the gap

  • Leave. Full-time and part-time employees get 4 weeks of annual leave, based on their ordinary hours (Fair Work), plus a leave loading the award sets at the greater of 17.5% or the penalty rates they'd have received (clause 21.3). Full-time employees get 10 sick days a year, pro-rata for part-time (Fair Work). The award's annual leave clause "does not apply to casual employees" (clause 21.1).
  • Super. "Generally, all employees are eligible for super guarantee. It doesn't matter if the employee is: full time, part time or casual" (ATO). It is 12% for 2026-27, paid for each payday under Payday Super.
  • Public holidays and workers' comp. Both add cost. Public holidays differ by state, and workers' comp premiums vary by state and insurer, so neither is in the figures below.

Cost per hour worked, weekday daytime

The kit's guide spreads 4 weeks' annual leave with 17.5% loading and 10 days' personal leave over the hours actually worked: (4 × 38 × 1.175 + 10 × 7.6) ÷ (52 × 38 − 4 × 38 − 10 × 7.6) = 14.6%. It assumes all personal leave is taken, which is cautious. Add that to the rate, then 12% super:

Two cautions: the part-time row applies the leave on-cost to the rate including the 15% allowance, so it may slightly overstate part-time cost, and none of these figures includes public holidays or workers' comp. Even so, at every level the part-timer costs about $2 an hour more than the casual for each hour worked.

Weekends widen it the other way

Penalty percentages are higher for casuals. Award Table 7 sets Saturday at 150% (full-time), 165% (part-time) and 175% (casual) of the minimum hourly rate, Sunday at 200%, 215% and 225%, and public holidays at 250%, 265% and 275%. For Level 1 that is $44.68 an hour part-time against $47.39 casual on a Saturday, and $58.22 against $60.93 on a Sunday. If most hours fall on weekends, run your own numbers.

Rules that apply to both

  • Minimum shifts. Part-time and casual cleaners must be rostered, and paid, for at least 2 consecutive hours at a location of up to 2,000 m², 3 from 2,000 m² and 4 from 5,000 m² (clause 13.5). One hour applies only to a lone cleaner at a stand-alone site of 300 m² or less where a longer shift across sites isn't practicable.
  • Overtime. For both, "All time worked in excess of 7.6 hours per day, five days per week or 38 hours in any week" is overtime (clause 19.2). Monday to Saturday it starts at 150% for part-time and 175% for casuals (Table 5).
  • Travel between sites is paid "as if they were working" (clause 17.12).
