Health Fund Provider Registration: Bupa, HCF, HBF and More

Current as of 17 November 2026. General information only, not legal or financial advice. Fund rules and forms change, so check each linked page before you apply.

Opening a physio, psychology, OT, podiatry, speech pathology, dietetics, chiro or osteo practice? Patients with extras cover will want to claim from their health fund. Here is which funds pick up your details from Medicare, which need something from you, and why on-the-spot claiming is a separate step.

Why fund recognition matters

Funds pay extras benefits only for providers they recognise. HCF says its members can only claim benefits for services given by recognised providers. Bupa says benefits depend on the practitioner being recognised by the fund and in private practice.

What the funds rely on first

Fund recognition sits on top of two things you set up earlier:

  • Professional registration. Australian Unity, for example, says all allied health providers must be registered with their Ahpra National Board or, where a service isn't governed by Ahpra, hold membership of the appropriate Allied Health Professions Australia (AHPA) association.
  • A Medicare provider number for each practice location. Services Australia says most applications take up to 28 days, and it gets a surge in applications between December and April. Several funds use your Medicare number as their trigger, so apply early.

Fund by fund

The funds split into two groups: those that take your details from Medicare, and those that need something from you. The table covers the funds checked for our paid guide, not every fund in Australia.

Four things to act on

  1. Send HCF its form if you will see HCF members. Its page lists all eight professions and doesn't say it takes your details from Medicare. HCF's recognition criteria include the Private Health Insurance (Accreditation) Rule 2011 and its Terms and Conditions for Recognised Providers of Extras Services, and it says meeting its requirements does not automatically confer recognition. The 'tell your professional association' route on HCF's page is for natural therapists, not allied health.
  2. Osteopaths: check with GMHBA how to be recognised, because osteopathy is not on its no-application list.
  3. Read Bupa's Ancillary Provider Terms before your first Bupa patient. Each provider must agree to abide by them, and Bupa says that if a provider doesn't keep to them, it may stop recognising that provider for paying benefits.
  4. Confirm the automatic funds. If a patient's claim is rejected in your first month, check with the fund that your details arrived.

Recognition is not on-the-spot claiming

Being recognised doesn't switch on terminal claiming by itself. Bupa says to contact HICAPS or HealthPoint directly to register your details, which are then sent to Bupa to enable electronic claiming at your location. HBF says that if you're registered with an electronic claiming terminal, you can claim electronically with it automatically.

HICAPS describes adding a new provider through HICAPS Digital: add the provider's details, submit a verification request, then track the application in HICAPS Digital.

ThroughWhen you can start claiming

HICAPS's page doesn't publish a processing time or price, so ask when you apply and allow time before opening. Each new practitioner needs their own verification request.

Keep your details current

  • Update provider details through Medicare. nib says it's important to keep them up to date with Medicare, as out-of-date details could affect your patients' ability to claim.
  • Check your receipts. nib says receipts must be on a letterhead or include your provider stamp.
  • Watch for changes to fund terms. HCF, for example, updated its Terms and Conditions for Recognised Providers from 1 September 2023.
  • Moving or adding a location means a new Medicare provider number. Funds that follow Medicare receive your new details from Medicare. If you're HCF-recognised, email HCF to update your details, and submit a HICAPS verification for each new number.

Fund networks are separate

Some funds also run contracted networks with set fees, such as Bupa's Members First Network and nib's First Choice Network. In nib's network, you agree to charge a set fee in return for having your services promoted to nib members. Both take expressions of interest, and nib's page names dentists, physiotherapists and optical providers.

Quick health fund checklist

  • Medibank, nib, Westfund: details confirmed as arrived from Medicare.
  • GMHBA / Frank: your modality is on its list, or you have checked how to be recognised.
  • Bupa: Ancillary Provider Terms read and agreed.
  • HCF: provider recognition form sent.
  • HBF: Medicare registration in place.
  • HICAPS verification submitted for each provider.
