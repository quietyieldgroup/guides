Payday Super 2026: Super Within 7 Business Days of Payday

Current as of 18 November 2026. General information only, not tax or financial advice. Rules and ATO pages change, so check the linked ATO pages, or ask a registered tax or BAS agent, before you rely on a date.

Since 1 July 2026, super is no longer a quarterly job. Under Payday Super, employers pay super for each payday, and it has to reach each employee's fund within 7 business days. If you run a small physiotherapy, psychology, OT, podiatry, speech pathology, dietetics, chiropractic or osteopathy practice with employees on the payroll, here is what changed, how to count the deadline, and the other payroll dates around it in 2026-27.

What Payday Super changed

The ATO's super guarantee page puts it in one line: "With the introduction of Payday Super, employers must pay super guarantee for each payday." From 1 July 2026, the minimum super guarantee is worked out as a percentage of each eligible employee's qualifying earnings, and the rate for 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027 is 12% (ATO: Super guarantee).

If the April to June 2026 payment is still outstanding, read the ATO's changeover page and get advice now rather than at year end.

Super within 7 days of payday? It's 7 business days

The ATO's test is exact: "Your super guarantee contribution is on time if it is received by your employee's super fund (with all the necessary information to allocate the contribution to the employee's member account) within 7 business days after paying your employee" (ATO: Payment deadlines for Payday Super). Three parts of that sentence matter:

  • Business days, not calendar days. Counting Monday to Friday, seven business days always takes in at least one weekend, so it is more than a calendar week.
  • Received by the fund. The test is when the money reaches the fund, not when you press pay. Allow for the time your payment takes to get there.
  • With the information the fund needs to allocate it. The contribution is on time when the fund receives it with all the details to put it in the employee's member account. Wrong or missing details put that at risk, so check each new employee's fund details when they start.

The same page says "You may have longer to make the contribution in some situations." This article doesn't cover which situations: check the ATO page if you think one applies.

Counting the 7 business days

Here is our count for some paydays in the coming weeks. We started from the day after payday, counted Monday to Friday only, and assumed no public holiday falls in between. If one does, check the ATO page before you rely on the date.

Notice the overlap if you pay weekly. The super for Thursday 19 November is still within its window when the next payday arrives on 26 November, so at any time one payday's super can be in progress while the next one starts.

A worked example

A made-up practice pays its receptionist weekly on Thursdays. On Thursday 26 November 2026 the receptionist is paid $1,500 of qualifying earnings. Super at 12% is $180, and by our count it must be in the receptionist's fund, with the details needed to allocate it, by Monday 7 December 2026. The next Thursday's pay starts its own 7-business-day count.

What it means for a small practice payroll

These are our suggestions, not ATO rules:

  • Pay super in the same pay run as wages. A habit of paying super monthly would miss the 7-business-day window for most weekly or fortnightly paydays.
  • Know your payroll's timing. Find out from your payroll software or bank how long a super payment takes to reach the fund, and send it with room to spare.
  • Check fund details at onboarding, so the fund has what it needs to allocate the first contribution.
  • Put "super each payday" in the practice calendar next to wages, so it doesn't depend on one person remembering.
