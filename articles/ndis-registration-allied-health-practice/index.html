NDIS Registration for an Allied Health Practice: Do You Need It?

Current as of 19 November 2026. General information only, not legal, tax or financial advice. NDIS rules change, so check the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission's linked pages, or ask the NDIA, before you decide.

Not every allied health practice has to register with the NDIS. Some providers must register. The Commission's list includes anyone who implements restrictive practices or provides supports to participants with NDIA-managed funding, but those aren't the only cases, so read the full list on its page. If nothing on it applies to you, you can choose: unregistered providers can still see self-managed and plan-managed participants. Here is what the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission says about each side, and what registration involves.

Registered or unregistered: what the Commission says

From the Commission's About registration page:

What the Commission says
You must register (the list includes)to be "a provider who implements restrictive practices (an implementing provider)", or to provide "supports or services to NDIS participants with NDIA-managed funding"
Unregistered providers"can only provide supports and services to self-manage or plan-managed NDIS participants" and "can't provide any of the supports where registration is mandatory"
Everyone not on the full list"Other providers can choose if they're registered."

So the questions are: which participants will you see, how is their plan managed, and do any of your supports need registration?

How to check your own supports

  1. Work out which supports you'd provide and claim for.
  2. Check the Commission's registration groups page. It says: "Look at the Registration groups or classes of support page, choose your registration group or groups, and check if you need to be registered."
  3. Ask the NDIA if you're unsure how to bill. The Commission says to contact the National Disability Insurance Agency for advice on "what line item is appropriate for your profession". Its contact details are on the Commission's page.

What registration involves

The Commission says registered providers have shown that:

  • "their business and key personnel have been checked to make sure they're suitable"
  • they've been audited to make sure they follow the rules and comply with the NDIS Practice Standards
  • their key personnel and staff in risk-assessed roles all have an NDIS worker screening clearance.

What it costs

"There is no cost to register with the NDIS Commission, but providers will need to pay for an approved quality auditor to complete an audit." The Commission says "The cost of an audit is dependent on the size and scale of your organisation and number of NDIS participants you support", and encourages providers to get quotes from several approved quality auditors (NDIS Commission: types of audits). We found no published audit price.

Your Ahpra registration may help. The Commission says "Many providers that need a verification audit have already met requirements of professional regulation. For example, they are certified through the Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency (AHPRA) or other professional bodies."

Why some practices register anyway

Beyond the mandatory cases, the Commission notes that "some support coordinators and other services prefer to connect NDIS participants to registered providers." Whether that matters depends on where your referrals come from.

Registration belongs to an ABN

This matters when you set up your structure, and when you buy or sell a practice. The Commission's page on buying or selling a registered NDIS business says:

  • "An NDIS registration is linked to a single ABN and is NOT transferrable to a different ABN."
  • "If the business needs to be linked to a different ABN, you need to complete a new registration application."
  • Some business structures, such as a sole trader, may not allow the ABN to be transferred to a new owner.

If you plan to move from sole trader to a company soon after opening, think about the order before you apply: a new ABN means a new NDIS registration application.

Where NDIS fits in the setup order

In the paid guide's suggested order, the NDIS decision comes near the end, after Ahpra registration, your ABN and your Medicare provider numbers. That order is our planning advice, not an NDIS rule. A few connections to know:

  • Claiming. HICAPS says new providers can start claiming straight away through HICAPS Digital with some payers, and its list includes the NDIS.
  • Ahpra. Keep your registration current: other health practitioners renew "by 30 November every year".
  • Renewing NDIS registration isn't covered in the paid guide. Check with the Commission.
