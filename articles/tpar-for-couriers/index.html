TPAR for Couriers and Road Freight: Paying Contractors

Current as of 19 November 2026. General information only, not tax advice. ATO pages change, so check the linked pages, or ask a registered tax or BAS agent, before you lodge.

If your business pays contractor drivers or subcontractors to deliver or haul for your customers, you may have to lodge a Taxable payments annual report (TPAR) each year. Here are the questions courier and road freight businesses ask, answered from the ATO's own pages.

Do I need to lodge a TPAR?

For both courier and road freight services, the ATO says you must lodge if all three of these apply (ATO: Courier services; ATO: Road freight services):

  1. your business provides the service to other people or businesses
  2. you paid contractors or subcontractors to provide the service on behalf of your business
  3. your business has an ABN.

If you paid no contractors in the year, the ATO says you should lodge a TPAR Non-lodgment advice instead.

Courier and freight together: combine them first

The ATO is clear on this one: "If your business provides both courier and road freight services, you must combine the payments you receive for both these services. Do this when you work out if you need to lodge a TPAR." It has applied since 1 July 2019.

Then run the 10% test. Add up what your business received for the services in the financial year, including work done by your employees as well as contractors, and divide by your business income (ATO: Work out if you need to lodge):

Total payments received for the services ÷ current or projected business income × 100 = %

  • 10% or more: you must lodge.
  • Less than 10%: you don't need to, though you can choose to lodge if you are under the threshold or unsure.

The ATO's example: a pizza shop earns $5,000 in delivery fees out of $100,000 of income. That is 5%, so it does not lodge.

What do I report for each driver?

For each contractor, the ATO says your TPAR must include (ATO: TPAR contractor details):

  • ABN, if known, and each ABN if a contractor's ABN changed during the year
  • business name or individual's name, and address
  • the gross amount paid for the year, including GST and any tax withheld
  • the total GST you paid them, and the total tax withheld where an ABN was not quoted.

The ATO also asks you to check that the ABN on each invoice matches the one on your record for that contractor, and to create a new contractor record if necessary. Check names, ABNs and GST registration on ABN Lookup.

Rules that catch transport businesses

  • Invoice includes labour and materials: report the total payment amount.
  • Cash basis: only report payments you made on or before 30 June.
  • Whole dollars only, excluding cents.
  • Labour-hire or on-hire drivers and your own employees are left out.
  • No ABN quoted: payers must withhold 47% unless an exception applies, such as a payment of $75 or less excluding GST (ATO: Statement by a supplier not quoting an ABN).

Your drivers see the figures: TPAR amounts "will now be pre-filled" in contractors' tax returns.

When is the 2026-27 TPAR due?

"A TPAR must be lodged by 28 August each year" (ATO: TPAR). For payments from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027 that is 28 August 2027, a Saturday. The ATO's general weekend rule would move it to Monday 30 August by our count, but the TPAR pages just say 28 August, so lodge by Friday 27 August 2027. Lodge online through business software, ATO online services or your registered tax or BAS agent: paper TPARs are no longer accepted. Late reports attract a failure to lodge penalty of one penalty unit for every 28 days (or part of 28 days) overdue, up to 5 units.
