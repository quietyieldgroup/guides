How to Quote Office Cleaning per Square Metre in Australia

Current as of 18 November 2026. General information only, not legal, tax, financial or employment advice. Award rates usually change every 1 July, so check the current Fair Work Ombudsman pay guide before you rely on any rate.

There is no official price per square metre for office cleaning in Australia, and this article doesn't quote what other cleaners charge. A rate per m² is something you work out, not something you look up: from how fast you clean, what each hour costs you, and the fixed costs of every visit. Here is the method, the award rules that change an office quote, and a worked example.

How to quote commercial cleaning per square metre

  1. Measure the floor area to be cleaned. You need it twice: for the hours, and for the award's minimum shift.
  2. Turn area into hours: area ÷ your cleaning rate (m² per hour), plus fixed minutes for each washroom, kitchen and other tasks such as bins, glass doors and lifts.
  3. Check the minimum shift. Part-time and casual cleaners must be paid at least the minimum for the site's size.
  4. Cost the visit: paid hours × your true cost per staff hour, plus travel, vehicle and supplies, plus an overhead share for each cleaning hour.
  5. Add your margin: divide the cost by (1 − margin).
  6. Add GST if you are registered. One-eleventh of what the customer pays is GST you pass on (business.gov.au).
  7. Only then divide by the floor area. The price per m² is a result of the quote, not a starting point.

The kit's Quick quote sheet starts with example timings: 150 m² an hour for general areas, 20 minutes per washroom, 15 per kitchen and 10 a visit for other tasks. They are not industry standards. Your rate depends on the fit-out, clutter and task list, so time a few of your own cleans and replace them.

Award rules that change an office quote

If you employ cleaners, check whether the Cleaning Services Award 2020 [MA000022] covers you. It covers "the business of providing cleaning services under a contract" (clause 4.2).

Minimum shifts are set by floor area

Under clause 13.5, part-time and casual cleaners must be rostered for a minimum shift set by the total cleaning area at the location, and paid for it "even if the employee works for a shorter time":

Total cleaning areaMinimum shift (part-time and casual)
Up to 2,000 m²2 consecutive hours
2,000 m² up to 5,000 m²3 consecutive hours
5,000 m² or more4 consecutive hours
Stand-alone site of 300 m² or less, one cleaner, and a longer shift across 2 or more locations isn't practicable1 hour

This is one reason small offices cost more per square metre. If a small office takes 1 hour 15 minutes and the 1-hour case doesn't apply, a part-time or casual cleaner is still paid 2 hours. Price the paid hours.

Shift times change the hourly rate

Level 1 hourly rates from the first full pay period starting on or after 1 July 2026, from the Fair Work Ombudsman pay guide published 24 June 2026 (FWO pay guides):

An early/late shift is a Monday to Friday shift that starts before 6am or finishes after 6pm, and the higher rate applies to the entire shift. So a weekday clean finishing at 6.30pm is paid at the early/late rate for every hour of it. A permanent night shift finishes after midnight and at or before 8am, and doesn't rotate with other shifts.

Worked example: a 400 m² office, 3 visits a week

These are the example values in the kit's spreadsheet, not a market price. Site: 400 m² at 150 m² an hour, 2 washrooms, 1 kitchen, 10 minutes of other tasks and $6 of supplies a visit. That is 3.75 hours a visit, above the 2-hour minimum. From the calculator's example inputs: a casual Level 1 cleaner with 10% of hours on Saturdays ($39.43 an hour including 12% super, the 2026-27 rate per the ATO), 30 minutes' paid travel, 20 km at $1.01/km, overheads of $1,100 a month over 40 jobs of 3 hours ($9.17 per cleaning hour), a 15% margin, and GST registration. What the example shows

  • About 35% of each visit's cost isn't time on site: travel, vehicle, supplies and overhead are $80.29 of $228.15. They come with every visit, so a smaller site pushes the price per m² up.
  • The 67 cents belongs to this site, these timings and this roster. Quote every site from its own area, fixed tasks, minimum shift and travel.
  • For an after-hours office, use your real share of early/late or night hours. The example's Saturday share is just the kit's default input.

Agree the scope, then invoice it properly

The kit's office checklist sets each task against a frequency (each visit, weekly, monthly or as agreed), from bins, washrooms and restocking to interior windows, carpet spot-cleaning and hard-floor machine scrubbing. Before the first clean, ask about site induction, sign-in, alarm codes, after-hours access and hazards.

  • If you're registered for GST, invoices must be tax invoices, and from $1,000 must also show the buyer's identity or ABN (ATO: tax invoices). The example's monthly invoice is over that.
  • Award rates usually change from 1 July each year, so say in your terms when you can review your price and how much notice you give.
  • If you pay contractors to clean, you may need to lodge a TPAR by 28 August each year (ATO).
