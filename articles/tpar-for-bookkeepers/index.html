TPAR for Bookkeepers and BAS Agents: Client Checklist

Current as of 19 November 2026. General information only, not tax advice. ATO pages change, so check the linked pages before you lodge for a client.

If you prepare Taxable payments annual reports (TPARs) for clients, the report is only as good as the payment records they kept through the year. Here are the questions bookkeepers and BAS agents ask, answered from the ATO's own pages, plus a way to get clients recording payments in one layout from July.

Which clients need to lodge?

Clients who pay contractors for any of the six TPRS services: building and construction, cleaning, courier, road freight, information technology (IT), and security, investigation or surveillance (ATO: Work out if you need to lodge). There are two tests:

ClientTest
Primarily in building and constructionMust lodge if it pays contractors for building and construction services and has an ABN. "Primarily" means 50% or more of income or activity from building and construction this year, or 50% or more of income last year.
Cleaning, courier, road freight, IT, securityLodges if payments received for the service are 10% or more of business income. Courier and road freight payments are combined first. Under 10%, the client can choose to lodge.
Paid no contractorsThe ATO says to lodge a Non-lodgment advice. One form can cover several years, and can say the client won't need to lodge in future.

What to collect from each client

For each contractor, the TPAR needs (ATO: TPAR contractor details):

  • ABN, if known, and each ABN if it changed during the year
  • business or individual's name, and address
  • gross amount paid for the year, including GST and any tax withheld; total GST paid; total tax withheld where no ABN was quoted, all in whole dollars only.

The ATO adds that it may ask for extra information about contractors: phone number, email address and bank account details if they're paid by electronic transfer. Ask clients to check the ABN on each invoice against their record, and to create a new contractor record if necessary.

Rules clients get wrong

  • Labour and materials on one invoice: report the total.
  • Cash basis: only payments made on or before 30 June.
  • Leave out materials only, incidental labour, labour hire, employees, payments within a consolidated group, and private and domestic projects (ATO: Payments to report).
  • No ABN quoted: 47% withholding unless an exception applies. Report the withheld amount in the TPAR or the NAT 3448 form, "not both". The same payments page also lists contractors who do not quote an ABN among payments not reported, so read it against the client's facts.
  • Overseas IT contractors: the IT page says "There is no exclusion based on the location or tax residency of the payee", while the payments page lists payments to foreign residents among those not reported, with exceptions. Read both pages for the client's facts.

Lodging for clients

  • Agents without business software use Online services for agents.
  • Software must be SBR-enabled, or able to create a TPAR data file to the ATO's specification.
  • Paper is gone. The ATO told agents on 3 August 2026: "All TPAR lodgments must be made through electronic channels."
  • The registered-agent lodgment program page gives the same due date: 28 August.

For 2026-27, 28 August 2027 is a Saturday. The ATO's general weekend rule would make it Monday 30 August by our count, but the TPAR pages just say 28 August, so plan client lodgments for Friday 27 August 2027 at the latest.

Fixing a lodged TPAR

From the ATO's amend a lodged TPAR page:

  • Wrong amounts: lodge an amendment. The details must be exactly as they appeared in the original, except the amended amounts, in whole dollars.
  • Wrong details only (not amounts): no amended report; send the corrected details online.
  • A payee left out: "To add a new payee, you need to lodge another TPAR".
  • An amendment may take up to 28 days to process.

Accuracy matters more now: the ATO says TPAR amounts "will now be pre-filled" in contractors' tax returns.

Clients who are overdue

The failure to lodge on time penalty covers TPARs: one penalty unit for every 28 days (or part) overdue, up to 5 units, multiplied by 2 for medium withholders and 5 for large ones. In March 2025 the ATO said that from 22 March it would apply penalties to businesses that hadn't lodged TPARs from 2024 or earlier and had been sent 3 reminder letters, and that the year before it had issued more than 11,000 businesses with about $18 million in penalties.
