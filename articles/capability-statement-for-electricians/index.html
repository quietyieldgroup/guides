Capability Statement for Electrical Contractors in Queensland

Current as of 19 November 2026. General information, not advice: check licence and insurance requirements with the Electrical Safety Office (in Queensland) or your state's electrical licensing body, and your insurer or broker. Each buyer sets its own requirements, and its quote or tender documents always come first. The licence rules below are Queensland's; other states differ.

An electrician's capability statement should answer three questions up front: are you licensed, are you insured to the buyer's level, and have you done work like this before? Answer them in one place: Queensland's state development department says a capability statement "helps the procurement officer decide if you are the right fit for the job". Here are the questions electrical contractors ask about writing one.

What licence details go in?

Your contractor licence, with its number, issuing body and expiry. The Electrical Safety Office's contractor licence page (on the WorkSafe Queensland website) says: "You must hold an electrical contractor licence to perform electrical work for others as a sole trader, partnership or company", and "Electrical contractor licences are issued for one year" (WorkSafe Qld: electrical contractor licences). So update the expiry date on your statement at each renewal.

  • Issuer: the Electrical Safety Office, which runs Queensland's electrical licensing.
  • Key people: applicants nominate at least one qualified technical person (QTP) and one qualified business person (QBP), who can be the same person. Use those terms for the licence holder's role.
  • Your electricians' own licences belong in a separate line. Don't show a worker's personal licence as if it were the company's contractor licence.

How much public liability do we need?

At least the licence minimum. The same page sets the cover for a contractor licence: it "must include public and products liability with: a limit of indemnity of at least $5,000,000". Buyers can ask for more: Local Buy, owned by the Local Government Association of Queensland, says "the mandatory minimum level of insurance cover for all Local Buy Suppliers is $20M Public Liability Insurance." Read each request for its own figure.

List each policy with its type, insurer, sum insured and expiry. Other lines buyers look for:

  • Workers' compensation: WorkCover Queensland says "If you employ workers in Queensland, you must have workers' compensation insurance."
  • Professional indemnity "covers mistakes, neglect or breaches of contract that result in a loss for your client" (business.gov.au). No design work? Our kit's electrician example lists it as "Not held", with the reason, rather than leaving a gap.
  • Certificates of currency: Business Queensland says a customer "may request a certificate of currency to prove that you have the required insurance cover. This is typical in a business-to-business sales scenario, such as a tender."

What about safety?

If you do high risk construction work, say you use safe work method statements. In Queensland a SWMS must be prepared before the work starts and, on a construction project, a copy given to the principal contractor (WorkSafe Qld: SWMS). Add one safety fact you can prove, such as your lost-time injury record, and how you check quality.

How do we show past jobs?

Three jobs most like the buyer's: client, location, value, completion date, what you did, and a result the client would confirm. Queensland's department prefers specifics: rather than "The required goods were delivered in full and on time", write "We delivered the required (items, quantity) on the required date of (date) which ensured project completion within timeframes."

The electrician in our kit's examples (a made-up business) writes its school switchboard job's result as "Power back on 4 days before staff returned. No defects at handover." Its overview gives checkable facts: 9 staff, 4 service vans on the road each day, and a 2-hour call-out for priority faults within 60 km of its yard.

Can we claim local, small or regional?

Only in the buyer's terms, and only what you can prove. The Queensland Procurement Policy 2026 defines a small business as "those businesses employing less than 20 people", a local workforce as workers living "within a 250 km radius" of your place of business who deliver the work, and a regional business as a Queensland supplier with its main business address on the ABR outside south-east Queensland. Toowoomba is on the policy's list of areas outside south-east Queensland. Be careful with promises: in state contracts, specific supplier commitments made in an offer are written into the contract, with "reporting on those commitments, where required".

Is the job too big for us?

Business Queensland warns: "Sometimes if the contract value is more than 20% of business turnover, that could count against you in the assessment of your tender response." If a job is a stretch, say how you will cover it: extra crew, hired plant or a named subcontractor.
