Ahpra Renewal Date 2026: Allied Health Registration Guide

Current as of 18 November 2026. General information only, not legal advice. Registration rules and dates change, so check the linked official pages and your National Board before you rely on a date.

If you are a physiotherapist, psychologist, occupational therapist, podiatrist, chiropractor or osteopath, your Ahpra registration renewal date is 30 November, every year. Here is the full renewal timeline, what happens if you miss it, what you declare when you renew, and the other dates a small allied health practice has to keep current.

Ahpra renewal date for allied health

Ahpra's renewal page says: "Other health practitioners renew their registration by 30 November every year" (Ahpra: Registration renewal). Its 30 November list includes chiropractors, occupational therapists, osteopaths, physiotherapists, podiatrists and psychologists. It applies to general, specialist and non-practising registration, which all "renew online at the same time each year". In 2026, 30 November is a Monday.

Speech pathology and dietetics are not among Ahpra's 15 National Boards, so use your professional association's dates instead. Keep that membership current: Services Australia says you're not eligible to claim Medicare benefits if your membership or registration has been suspended or has expired (Services Australia: Eligibility for health professionals).

The early October and early November dates are worked out from what Ahpra says, not printed by it. The hardship date assumes no public holiday falls in the 15 working days, so check yours.

What a lapsed registration means

Ahpra says that once your registration lapses, "your name will be removed from the public register" and you "will not be able to use your profession's protected title". To resume practice later you have to "lodge a completely new application for registration".

What you declare when you renew

When you renew, Ahpra says you must declare that you meet your National Board's registration standards, including:

  • professional indemnity insurance (cover that meets your Board's standard, for all aspects of your practice; you declare you won't practise without it)
  • recency of practice (that you have worked the required minimum practice hours)
  • continuing professional development (CPD), including the minimum hours for your profession, in the previous registration period
  • criminal history.

You also need to tell Ahpra about any health impairments you haven't reported before that may affect your ability to practise safely.

These are declarations, but they get checked. "National Boards routinely audit a randomly selected number of practitioners each year", and you need to be able to back up what you declared. Ahpra's examples of evidence are a certificate of currency for your indemnity insurance, and payslips or letters from employers for recency. Keep your CPD records too. See Ahpra's indemnity insurance and CPD standards pages for your Board's details.

After you renew

  • Check your details on the public register about an hour after you submit.
  • Download your certificate of registration from your Ahpra portal. Ahpra says it "does not send out certificates".

Provisional or limited registration

These don't follow 30 November. Provisional and limited registration renew on the anniversary of when they were first granted. The reminder email comes about three to four months before expiry, and you must apply before your expiry date or in the one-month grace period after it. Provisional registration can only be renewed twice; limited registration, three times.
