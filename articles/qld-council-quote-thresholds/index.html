Queensland Council Quote Thresholds 2026: How Small Firms Win

Current as of 17 November 2026. General information only, not legal advice. Council thresholds are indexed each 1 July and councils update their policies at different times, so check your council's current procurement policy before you rely on a figure.

Most council work sized for a small trade or service firm never appears as a public tender. It goes out as invited quotes, or to firms already on a list. Knowing the dollar thresholds tells you which jobs you will see advertised and which ones you only get if a buyer can find you.

The default Queensland council thresholds

Every Queensland council except Brisbane City Council buys under the same law, the Local Government Regulation 2012. (Brisbane has its own Act and Regulation.) The Regulation sets two thresholds. A 2025 amendment raised them from $15,000 and $200,000 to $21,000 and $280,000, in force from 12 December 2025, and they are indexed each 1 July. After the 1 July 2026 indexation, Quilpie Shire Council's procurement policy shows $21,900 and $292,000, citing sections 223B and 225 of the Regulation.

Watch for out-of-date pages: when checked, at least one council's procurement page still showed the old $15,000 and $200,000 figures. Always check the date on the policy.

Some big councils set higher bands

Councils can use the Regulation's "strategic contracting procedures" and publish their own bands. Two examples, in the councils' own terms (ex GST):

CouncilBands
LoganUp to $50,000: a quotation "from a local business in the first instance". $50,000 to $500,000: "a minimum of 3 quotations only from local business in the first instance. Quotations must be invited through VendorPanel." Above $500,000: tender or expression of interest.
Moreton BayUnder $25,000: one written quote (minimum) from a local business first, where suitable. $25,000 to $250,000: two written quotes. $250,000 to $500,000: three written quotes, local businesses first. $500,000 or more: tenders, which may include a 15 percent local business and local benefit weighting.

In these councils, jobs up to half a million dollars go to invited local firms first, where suitable. If you are local and registered on VendorPanel, you are in the pool. If you are not registered, buyers can't find you there.

State agencies use different rules

State departments and agencies follow the Queensland Procurement Policy 2026, which started on 1 January 2026. Its figures include GST. The rules that matter most to a small firm:

  • Agencies may go to a single supplier when the value is under $50,000 including GST, so being known and registered matters.
  • For routine (low value, low risk) buying, agencies "must invite at least one local supplier or small and family business or a Queensland regional business to respond", or document why that isn't practical.
  • Agencies must publish basic details of awarded contracts of $10,000 and above, including the supplier and value. Use these to see who wins what in your area.
  • Where you were invited and responded, agencies must offer feedback. Always take the debrief.

Small businesses with fewer than 20 employees that supply the state are paid within 20 calendar days under the On-time Payment Policy, once registered on the On-time Payment Small Business Register. Some departments have exemptions, so check the agency you supply.

How small businesses win council work in Queensland

1. Get findable on VendorPanel

State agencies and most councils now use VendorPanel, reached through supply.qld.gov.au, where supplier profiles are free. The steps from that portal:

  1. Choose Create Profile on supply.qld.gov.au.
  2. Select your supply categories. The portal advises registering "into all supply categories (Marketplace Lists) that match your business capability".
  3. Register as a supplier and request an invitation.
  4. Set your regions of service and switch on notifications for quotes and tenders under Options and Settings.

Councils pick suppliers to invite from these categories. If you are not in the category, you can't be picked. Fill in the whole profile too: buyers search it.

Brisbane City Council is not on VendorPanel. It uses its own SAP Ariba supplier portal, and says to allow 2 business days for it to process your registration.

2. Get onto the lists

  • Local Buy, owned by the LGAQ, runs pre-qualified arrangements that councils use. You join by tender in windows that open on 1 February, 1 May, 1 August and 1 November. Its fees page lists a $500 initial and $250 annual renewal supplier verification fee (ex GST) per arrangement, waived for businesses that meet its criteria, such as revenue under $500,000 a year or being a sole trader, with a head office in Queensland, Tasmania or the NT. Every Local Buy supplier needs at least $20 million public liability cover.
  • QBuild, for state building maintenance and minor works, has a free register that stays active for 2 years. It usually asks for low-value quotes from a shortlist on that register.
  • Council panels. Many councils run their own preferred or prequalified supplier lists. At Southern Downs, for example, you join through the official tender process, and lists can be refreshed about once a year. Ask each council's procurement team when its trade panels next open.

A panel is permission to be asked, not a promise of work. Once you're on, keep reminding buyers you exist.

3. Make yourself known

  • Read the procurement page of every council you can service, and note its bands, any local-first rules and its panels.
  • Go to supplier briefings where councils run them.
  • Send the procurement team a short introduction with a one-page capability statement.
  • Quote well on small jobs. A clean, on-time quote at $15,000 is how you get asked again at $150,000.

And bid carefully: Business Queensland's advice is "If you can't deliver, don't bid".
