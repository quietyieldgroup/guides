Opening an OT Private Practice in Australia: 2026 Checklist

Current as of 19 November 2026. General information only, not legal, tax or financial advice. Rules, forms and processing times change, so check each linked official page (Ahpra, Services Australia, the ATO, DVA, the NDIS Commission and the funds) before you apply.

Opening your own occupational therapy practice, whether from rooms or on the road? Before your first client can claim, there is a chain of registrations to get through, and for many OTs an NDIS decision as well. Here are the questions OTs ask, answered from the official pages.

Is my registration ready?

You are registered through the Occupational Therapy Board of Australia (Ahpra). Ahpra says other health practitioners, which covers OTs, "renew their registration by 30 November every year": this year, Monday 30 November 2026 (provisional or limited registration renews on the anniversary it was granted instead). In December's one-month late period you pay a late fee on top; after that your registration lapses and your name is removed from the register (Ahpra: Registration renewal). Services Australia says you're not eligible to claim Medicare benefits if your registration has expired, so renew first, and check your professional indemnity insurance covers "all aspects" of your practice, as your Board's standard requires.

What order do the registrations go in?

  1. Ahpra registration current.
  2. ABN, a business name if you won't trade under your own name, and GST if it applies.
  3. PRODA, your individual government login, then HPOS linked inside it.
  4. A Medicare provider number for each practice location.
  5. Then DVA, the health funds, claiming, workers' compensation and NDIS registration if you need or choose it.

Medicare: how long, and where?

Occupational therapists are on Services Australia's list of allied health professionals who may qualify (Services Australia: Eligibility). Most provider number applications take up to 28 days, some longer, with a surge between December and April. You need a unique number for each place of practice, and you can't claim a Medicare benefit until the number for that location is issued. Apply before your start date.

Mobile practice from home

Services Australia can't suppress a provider number address, so a home address used as a practice location may be publicly available. If home is your only practice location and you only provide mobile or telehealth consultations, an alternative address such as a PO Box registered to you may be recorded, on a signed request.

Is occupational therapy GST-free?

Occupational therapy is on the ATO's list of GST-free health services, where the service is performed by a recognised professional and is generally accepted in the profession as necessary for the client's treatment (ATO: GST and other health services). If an assistant works with your clients, the ATO sets several conditions for their part to count as your GST-free service, including that it is billed in your name, you accept full responsibility and you supervise the assistant directly: check the full list on the ATO page. Ask your accountant whether to register for GST.

DVA and the health funds

DVA registers occupational therapists (general or mental health) automatically when they get a Medicare provider number. Lymphoedema providers, a group DVA limits to physiotherapists and OTs, must register directly. Form D9537 (ABN and GST status) is a separate step.

Workers' compensation clients

  • Victoria: complete WorkSafe's registration form with your Occupational Therapy Board of Australia registration number, and keep at least $1 million of professional indemnity cover at all times (WorkSafe Victoria: occupational therapy).
  • New South Wales: SIRA requires approval for some treating allied health disciplines (its list includes physiotherapists, chiropractors, psychologists and accredited exercise physiologists), and says those not on its list don't need approval. Check SIRA's current list before you treat NSW workers.
  • Other states: check your state scheme's provider page.

Do I need to register for the NDIS?

Not always. The NDIS Commission says:

  • Unregistered providers can only provide supports to self-managed or plan-managed participants, and can't provide any supports where registration is mandatory.
  • Providers must register if they provide specialist disability accommodation, specialist behaviour support services (as a specialist behaviour support provider or a provider who implements restrictive practices), supports to participants with NDIA-managed funding, plan management, supported independent living, NDIS digital platform services, or residential aged care with NDIS participants. If you write or deliver behaviour support plans, check the specialist behaviour support rules first.
  • Other providers can choose. Check the registration groups page for your supports.

Registration means checks on your business and key personnel, an audit against the NDIS Practice Standards, and worker screening clearances for key personnel and staff in risk-assessed roles. There is no cost to register with the Commission, but you pay an approved quality auditor. Many providers needing a verification audit have already met professional regulation requirements, for example through Ahpra. Audit cost depends on your size and participant numbers, so get quotes from several auditors. One reason to register anyway: some support coordinators prefer to connect participants to registered providers.
