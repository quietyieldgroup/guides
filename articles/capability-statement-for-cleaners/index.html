Capability Statement for Cleaning Companies: What to Include

Current as of 19 November 2026. General information, not advice: check licence and insurance requirements with your state's licensing body (ABLIS can help you find it) and your insurer or broker. Each buyer sets its own requirements, and its quote or tender documents always come first.

Going for an office, school or council cleaning contract? The tender may ask for a capability statement as well as your price. Queensland's state development department puts it simply: "A capability statement is like a resume for your business and helps the procurement officer decide if you are the right fit for the job" (3 essential steps). Here are the questions commercial cleaning firms ask about writing one.

When will a buyer ask for one?

With tenders, most often. Business Queensland lists what tenders must include to be considered, among them "your ABN", "a company profile and capability statement", "whether you propose to subcontract" and "applicable insurances". It adds: "Late or incomplete tender responses are usually excluded from consideration barring exceptional, pre-agreed circumstances."

Supplier portals count too. Sunshine Coast Council notes: "Buyers can also search suppliers so a robust profile ensures that your business is well represented." Put the same facts in your profile as in your statement.

We have no trade licence. What goes in that box?

First check whether your state or the work needs one: the national ABLIS tool helps you "find out which licences you need to start, run and grow your business". Then list what you actually hold and can prove, instead of leaving the box empty or padding it.

The commercial cleaner example in our kit (a made-up business) fills the box with four lines it could back with records: police checks for all staff before starting, renewed every 3 years; supplier profiles on the state and two council portals; a written induction for every new site; and a chemical register with safety data sheets for every product. Those are its own practices, not rules for every cleaner: write yours.

Do we say we use subcontractors?

Yes, if you do. Tenders ask "whether you propose to subcontract". In your core services, say which services you subcontract rather than presenting them as your own staff's work.

What insurance details do buyers want?

Figures, not "fully insured". For each policy list the type, insurer, sum insured and expiry. From business.gov.au:

  • Public liability "covers you if someone dies, gets injured or has their property damaged".
  • Workers' compensation: "Employers must get workers compensation insurance from an authorised insurer." And: "The laws vary in each state and territory."
  • Professional indemnity "covers mistakes, neglect or breaches of contract that result in a loss for your client". If you don't hold it, write "not held".

Buyers set their own minimums. One published example: Local Buy, owned by the Local Government Association of Queensland, says "the mandatory minimum level of insurance cover for all Local Buy Suppliers is $20M Public Liability Insurance." Keep certificates of currency ready. Business Queensland says a customer "may request a certificate of currency to prove that you have the required insurance cover", and that it "can usually be provided to you immediately via email".

How do we show past contracts?

With numbers a referee could confirm. Western Australia's Small Business Development Corporation (SBDC) says: "List any significant projects or achievements (including dollar value)" (WA SBDC). Compare:

"Client was very happy""3 campuses, 5 nights a week, 2,400 m²; audit score 95% or better for 18 months."
"Reliable service""4 suites, daily clean plus fortnightly deep clean; zero missed shifts in 2025."
"Experienced in builders' cleans""3 floors, 1,800 m², cleaned in 2 nights to meet a handover date."

Pick the three contracts most like the buyer's: same kind of site, similar size. The SBDC also suggests grouping experience "by location or service". For referees, Business Queensland says "Use referees that know your products or services": ask each one first and tell them which contract.

One page or two?

There is no official length. The SBDC says statements "can be a few pages or much more complex documents. Your statement will depend on the audience's needs". Our suggestion for a firm of 1 to 20 staff: one page for introductions and supplier profiles, two for tenders and panels. If the buyer sets a page limit or its own form, theirs wins.

Keep it current

Queensland's department says to "ensure statement is updated at least once per year", and warns that "Many submissions fall short because of simple mistakes such as missing mandatory requirements, leaving questions unanswered, or failing to provide supporting evidence." Answer the buyer's forms first, then attach the statement.
