Opening a Psychology Private Practice: 2026 Setup Checklist

Current as of 19 November 2026. General information only, not legal, tax or financial advice. Rules, forms and processing times change, so check each linked official page (Ahpra, Services Australia, the ATO, DVA, the funds and the OAIC) before you apply.

Moving from employment or a group practice into your own psychology private practice? Before your first client can claim, there is a chain of registrations to get through. Here are the questions psychologists ask, answered from the official pages.

Is my registration ready?

You are registered through the Psychology Board of Australia (Ahpra). Ahpra says other health practitioners, which covers psychologists, "renew their registration by 30 November every year": this year, Monday 30 November 2026 (provisional or limited registration renews on the anniversary it was granted instead). Renewing in December's one-month late period costs a late fee on top; after that, registration lapses and your name is removed from the register (Ahpra: Registration renewal). Services Australia says you're not eligible to claim Medicare benefits if your registration has been suspended or has expired, so renew first.

Your professional indemnity insurance must meet your Board's standard "for all aspects of their practice": confirm it covers private practice.

What order do the registrations go in?

  1. Ahpra registration current.
  2. ABN, a business name if you won't trade under your own name, and GST if it applies.
  3. PRODA, your individual government login.
  4. HPOS, linked in PRODA, where you apply for provider numbers.
  5. A Medicare provider number for each practice location.
  6. Then DVA, the health funds, claiming and workers' compensation.

Medicare: how long, and how many numbers?

Psychologists are on Services Australia's list of allied health professionals who may qualify (Services Australia: Eligibility):

  • Most provider number applications take up to 28 days; some take longer.
  • Applications surge between December and April and are processed in order of receipt.
  • You need a unique number for each place of practice, and you can't claim a Medicare benefit until the number for that location is issued.
  • Apply before your proposed location start date.

This page doesn't cover which Medicare items you can bill or the referral each one needs: check MBS Online and your professional association for that.

Telehealth from home

Services Australia can't suppress a provider number address, so a home address used as a practice location may be publicly available. If your home is your only practice location and you only provide telehealth or mobile consultations, an alternative address such as a PO Box registered to you may be recorded instead, on a signed request.

Is psychology GST-free?

Psychology is on the ATO's list of GST-free health services, where the service is performed by a recognised professional and is generally accepted in the profession as necessary for the client's treatment (ATO: GST and other health services). Ask your accountant whether you should register for GST.

DVA and the health funds

DVA registers psychologists and clinical psychologists automatically when they get a Medicare provider number. Neuropsychologists must register directly. DVA's form D9537 (ABN and GST status) is a separate step.

Workers' compensation clients

  • New South Wales: psychologists need SIRA approval to be treated as appropriately qualified to provide services in the workers compensation system.
  • Victoria: WorkSafe asks psychologists to complete its registration form with their Psychology Board of Australia registration number, and to keep at least $2 million of professional indemnity cover at all times (WorkSafe Victoria: psychology).

Privacy and advertising from day one

The Privacy Act covers some small business operators with turnover of $3 million or less, including a private sector health service provider, so a private practice is likely covered: confirm with the OAIC or your adviser. If it applies, the Australian Privacy Principles require a clearly expressed, up-to-date privacy policy covering what personal information you collect and hold, how and why, how people can access and correct it, how to complain, and any overseas disclosure. It will usually be on your website.

Ahpra's advertising rules ban testimonials about clinical aspects (symptoms, diagnosis, treatment, outcome), including on your practice's social media. If you use the title "Dr" and aren't a medical practitioner, state your profession, as in Ahpra's own example "Dr Lee (osteopath)" (Ahpra: advertising requirements).

And if you don't have access to Medicare, you must let clients know before you treat them that they won't get Medicare benefits for your services. Services Australia says it is an offence not to.
