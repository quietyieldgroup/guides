Capability Statement Template Australia: Qld Council Tenders

Current as of 17 November 2026. General information only, not legal or insurance advice. Rules change, so check the linked official pages before you rely on them.

A capability statement is your business résumé for a buyer. For a small trade or service firm chasing council and state government work in Queensland, it goes out with tender responses and with your introduction to a council's procurement team. Here is where it gets used, what goes in it, and how to back every line.

Where a capability statement gets used

WhereWhy it matters
Tender responsesBusiness Queensland's tendering tips list what tenders commonly ask for: "your ABN", "a company profile and capability statement", "whether you propose to subcontract", "applicable insurances", and "a description of any variations you propose".
Introducing yourself to councilsMuch council work goes out as invited quotes. Under the default rules in the Local Government Regulation 2012, councils invite written quotes from at least 3 suppliers for medium-sized contracts, and the council chooses who. A short introduction with a one-page capability statement helps its buyers know you exist.
Your supplier profileState agencies and most councils use VendorPanel, reached through supply.qld.gov.au. Sunshine Coast council notes: "Buyers can also search suppliers so a robust profile ensures that your business is well represented." Put the same facts in your profile.

What goes in a capability statement

The layout below summarises the one in the paid guide, which is its own suggestion, not an official government format. Three ground rules: keep it to one or two pages, put facts before adjectives, and tailor the relevant experience to each buyer.

PartWhat to include
Who you areBusiness and trading name, ABN, main business address as shown on the Australian Business Register, contact details, and the towns or councils you service.
What you doTwo sentences covering your trade, the type of work and your typical job size, then up to six core services.
Licences and registrationsLicence numbers and classes, plus the supplier registrations you hold, such as VendorPanel, QBuild, Local Buy and council panels.
InsurancesYour public and products liability limit, workers' compensation, and professional indemnity if you have it.
Relevant experienceThe three jobs most like the buyer's work: client, scope, value and year.
People and plantStaff numbers, key people and their tickets, apprentices, and your vehicles, machines and specialist gear.
Safety and qualitySafe work method statements (SWMS) in use, your WHS system, your incident record and any certifications held.
Local and community factsOnly what is true and checkable: a Queensland or regional address, how far your staff live from your base, local suppliers you use, apprentices.
Why usThree short points, such as response time, a local depot, after-hours availability or a specialist skill.

Back every line with a document

A buyer can ask you to prove any claim. Keep the evidence in one folder, as PDFs, because invited quotes can come with short deadlines.

ClaimWhat the official source says
QBCC licenceNeeded to contract for building work "valued over $3,300", and for work of any value involving drainage, plumbing and drainage, gas fitting, fire protection and other listed classes (QBCC).
Electrical contractor licenceNeeded "to perform electrical work for others as a sole trader, partnership or company". It is issued for one year and requires at least $5 million public and products liability (WorkSafe Queensland).
Public liability limitKnow your number. Local Buy requires at least $20 million public liability cover for all its suppliers (Local Buy).
Workers' compensation"If you employ workers in Queensland, you must have workers' compensation insurance" (WorkCover Queensland).
Certificates of currencyA customer "may request a certificate of currency to prove that you have the required insurance cover. This is typical in a business-to-business sales scenario, such as a tender" (Business Queensland). Brokers can usually email one straight away.
SafetyA SWMS for high risk construction work must be prepared before the work starts and given to the principal contractor (WorkSafe Queensland).

For state agency work, rule 13 of the Queensland Procurement Policy 2026 says agencies "should consider" requesting "evidence of insurances held or certifications, reference reports only if the supplier is shortlisted or successful." So have it ready, even if it isn't asked for up front.

For referees, Business Queensland says "Use referees that know your products or services". Line up 3, with their permission, and keep notes on 5 recent comparable jobs: client, scope, value, dates and photos.

Local and small business claims: keep them checkable

The state's policy favours local, small and regional firms, but only if you claim it accurately. Its glossary sets the tests:

TermDefinition (QPP 2026)
Queensland supplier"A supplier with an Australian Business Number (ABN) registered in the Australian Business Register with a main business address in Queensland."
Small business"those businesses employing less than 20 people"
Local supplier"A local supplier is a Queensland supplier that maintains a local workforce."
Local workforceWorkers "whose usual place of residency is located within a 250 km radius of the supplier's permanent place of business or established presence" and who help deliver the work.

State contracts must incorporate "specific supplier commitments made during the offer or negotiation process and reporting on those commitments, where required." What you promise, you will report on. For the "purposeful public procurement" criterion in state tenders, Business Queensland advises suppliers to "provide concrete examples of what you're already doing or what you can commit to delivering" and to "be specific and realistic about your commitments."

A line about being a proud local business that "will employ local apprentices" has no facts, and "will employ" can become a contract term you can't meet. A strong line states what a buyer can check: your main business address, staff numbers and where they live, apprentices employed now, and your local materials spend.

Send it, then keep it current

  1. Write it, then have someone outside the business read it.
  2. Read each council's procurement page: note its bands, local-first rules, panels and contact address.
  3. Email each council's procurement team a short introduction with your capability statement attached.
  4. Go to supplier briefings where councils run them. Moreton Bay, for example, hosts sessions where businesses can "engage with procurement staff and buyers from Council".
  5. Update the statement with each new completed job, and renew certificates of currency before they expire.
