What to Include in a Capability Statement, With Examples

Current as of 18 November 2026. General information only, not legal or insurance advice. Each buyer sets its own requirements, and its quote or tender documents always come first.

Queensland's state development department puts it simply: "A capability statement is like a resume for your business and helps the procurement officer decide if you are the right fit for the job" (3 essential steps to crafting a winning capability statement). Here is what to include, what to leave out, and three short examples.

What to include in a capability statement

There is no official length. Western Australia's Small Business Development Corporation (SBDC) says statements "can be a few pages or much more complex documents. Your statement will depend on the audience's needs" (WA SBDC: How to develop a capability statement). Its guide splits the contents into a basic set and a more detailed set:

Basic statementMore detailed statement adds
Introduction and company profile; core competencies; management and team profile; major client list; description of products and/or services; contact detailsVision and mission statement; experience; awards and recognition; accreditations; quality assurance; governance and risk management practices; management systems; client testimonials

For a firm of 1 to 20 staff, we suggest one page for introductions, supplier profiles and your website, and two pages for tenders, panels and head contractors. If a buyer sets a page limit or its own form, theirs wins.

Capability statement examples

These three businesses are fictional: every name, number and result is made up.

Example 1: electrician (two pages)

The overview gives facts a buyer can check:

"We provide electrical maintenance, minor works and lighting upgrades for councils, schools, aged care and commercial sites across the Darling Downs. Typical jobs are $2,000 to $250,000. … 9 staff, 4 service vans on the road each day, and a 2-hour call-out for priority faults within 60 km of our yard."

Its insurance table lists professional indemnity as "Not held", with the reason ("No design work"), instead of padding.

Example 2: commercial cleaner (one page)

With no trade licence to show, the licences box lists police checks for all staff before starting, renewed every 3 years; a written induction for every new site; and a chemical register with safety data sheets for every product. Each project result is a number a referee could confirm: "3 campuses, 5 nights a week, 2,400 m²; audit score 95% or better for 18 months."

Example 3: landscape and civil firm (two pages)

Licence, council panel and supplier registrations sit in one table with numbers and expiry dates. Plant shows sizes ("Excavators: 1 x 1.7 t, 2 x 5 t, with tilt buckets"). Its depot is in south-east Queensland, so it says it does not claim regional status.

Tailor it in 20 minutes per bid

The SBDC: "Think about who your audience is and write specifically for them." Keep one-page and two-page base versions, then for each bid:

  1. Read the buyer's documents: criteria, page limits, forms, minimum insurance and licences.
  2. Put this buyer's kind of work first in your services, in their words.
  3. Swap in your three closest projects: same kind of work, similar size, similar client.
  4. Rewrite the overview's first line in the feature-and-benefit format.
  5. Match referees to those projects, and tell each one to expect a call.
  6. Check every expiry date, have someone else read it, and save it as a dated PDF named for the buyer.

Common mistakes

  • Not answering what was asked. Queensland's department says "Many submissions fall short because of simple mistakes such as missing mandatory requirements, leaving questions unanswered, or failing to provide supporting evidence" (Your roadmap to supply chain success). Answer the buyer's forms first.
  • Out of date. The department: "ensure statement is updated at least once per year". Better: after every finished job.
  • Never proofread. Get a second reader, and search for "[" to catch leftover template placeholders.
