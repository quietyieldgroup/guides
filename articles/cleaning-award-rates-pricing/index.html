Cleaning Services Award 2026 Rates: How Much to Charge

Current as of 17 November 2026. General information only, not legal, tax, financial or employment advice. Award rates usually change every 1 July. Check the current Fair Work Ombudsman pay guide before you rely on any rate.

There is no official "going rate" for cleaning in Australia, and this article doesn't quote one. What you can work out exactly is your floor: what an hour of cleaning costs you under the Cleaning Services Award, before you add overheads and profit. Price below that and every job loses money. Here are the 2026 rates, what they really cost per hour, and a worked example of turning them into a price.

Full-time weekly minimums are $1,028.90 (Level 1), $1,062.90 (Level 2) and $1,119.10 (Level 3). An early/late shift is a Monday to Friday shift that starts before 6am or finishes after 6pm, and the higher rate applies to the entire shift. The pay guide itself says: "If our information is inconsistent with the award, then the award applies."

Why part-time and casual rates are higher

  • Part-time cleaners get a 15% allowance on every ordinary hour worked (award clause 10.2).
  • Casuals get a 25% loading (clause 11.2), paid instead of leave.
  • For full-time staff, Saturday is 150% of the minimum hourly rate, Sunday 200% and public holidays 250%. Part-time and casual percentages are higher.

What an hour of cleaning really costs

The award rate is only the start. Full-time and part-time staff are paid for annual leave and personal leave, so every hour they actually clean has to carry that cost. On top of that, the ATO sets super guarantee at 12% for 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027, and under Payday Super employers must pay it for each payday.

For a Level 1 cleaner on weekday daytime hours, allowing for 4 weeks' annual leave with the award's 17.5% loading and 10 days' personal leave (a leave on-cost of 14.6%), plus 12% super:

These figures leave out public holidays and workers' compensation, so your real cost will be higher. Note that under this award a part-time cleaner can cost more per hour worked than a casual. Check the numbers before assuming part-time is cheaper.

The rules that make quotes lose money

  • Minimum shifts. Part-time and casual cleaners must be paid for at least 2 hours at a site of up to 2,000 m² (3 hours from 2,000 m², 4 from 5,000 m²), even if the job takes less (clause 13.5). There is a 1-hour minimum only in a narrow case for a lone cleaner at a small stand-alone site.
  • Travel time. Time travelling between workplaces is paid "as if they were working" (clause 17.12).
  • Weekend and shift work. A Saturday-heavy roster can add a lot to your average hourly cost.
  • Allowances. The award also has a vehicle allowance of $1.01 per km for an employee's own car, a broken shift allowance and a toilet cleaning allowance, among others.
  • GST. If you are registered, one-eleventh of what the customer pays is GST you pass on (business.gov.au). A $100 GST-inclusive price leaves you $90.91.

Fair Work has said that "the cleaning sector has a significant history of non-compliance with workplace laws". In its investigation of 17 Adelaide commercial cleaning companies, the most common breaches included failing to pay penalty rates and underpaying minimum hourly rates (media release, 13 January 2022). Those businesses were selected because of concerns raised, so it isn't a random sample. But a price that only covers the base hourly rate can't pay the award.

Margin, not mark-up

A 15% margin means $15 profit in every $100 of price (ex GST), so you divide cost by (1 − 0.15). Adding 15% to cost instead, a mark-up, gives only about a 13% margin.

If you clean yourself

If you have no employees, the award doesn't set your price, but it tells you what a competitor who employs staff must pay. Use the hourly amount you want to earn as your labour cost, and remember nobody pays your super or leave, so build them in. You must register for GST once your GST turnover reaches $75,000, within 21 days (ATO).
