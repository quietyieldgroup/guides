TPAR Due Date 2026: Who Needs to Lodge a TPAR

Current as of 18 November 2026. General information only, not tax advice. Rules and ATO pages change, so check the linked ATO pages, or ask a registered tax or BAS agent, before you lodge.

If your business pays contractors for certain services, you may have to lodge a Taxable payments annual report (TPAR) every year. Here is the due date, who has to lodge, what goes in it and how to lodge, from the ATO's own pages.

TPAR due date

The ATO's rule is short: "A TPAR must be lodged by 28 August each year" (ATO: Taxable payments annual report). Each report covers the financial year that ended on the 30 June before.

Financial yearPayments coveredTPAR due
2025-261 July 2025 to 30 June 202628 August 2026 (now passed)
2026-271 July 2026 to 30 June 202728 August 2027

28 August 2027 is a Saturday. The ATO's general rule is that when a due date falls on a weekend or public holiday, you can lodge on the first business day after without a penalty (ATO: Due dates for lodging and paying). By our count that is Monday 30 August 2027, but the TPAR pages just say 28 August, so the safe choice is Friday 27 August 2027.

Missed 28 August 2026?

TPARs attract the ATO's failure to lodge on time penalty: one penalty unit for every 28 days (or part of 28 days) overdue, up to 5 units, multiplied for medium and large withholders. In one ATO case study, a builder who missed the 28 August 2024 deadline got a failure to lodge penalty of $1,650. If you don't need to lodge at all, tell the ATO with a Non-lodgment advice (see below).

Who needs to lodge a TPAR

The TPAR is for businesses that pay contractors (including subcontractors and consultants) to provide services under the Taxable payments reporting system (TPRS). There are six TPRS services (ATO: Work out if you need to lodge a TPAR):

  • building and construction
  • cleaning
  • courier
  • road freight
  • information technology (IT)
  • security, investigation or surveillance.

Cleaning, courier, road freight, IT and security: the 10% test

For these five services, the ATO says you must lodge if all of these apply:

  1. your business provides the service to other people or businesses
  2. you paid contractors or subcontractors to provide the service on behalf of your business
  3. your business has an ABN.

If the service is only part of your business, add up what you received for it in the financial year (including work done by employees as well as contractors) and divide by your business income:

Payments received for the service ÷ current or projected business income × 100 = %

  • 10% or more: you must lodge a TPAR.
  • Less than 10%: you don't need to lodge, though you can choose to lodge if you are under 10% or unsure.
  • Trading the full year: use your current business income. Trading less than 12 months: use projected income for a full year.
  • If you provide both courier and road freight services, combine the payments for both before you do the test.

ATO examples: a building maintenance business received $10,000 for office cleaning out of $100,000 income. That is 10%, so it lodges, reporting the $5,000 paid to cleaning contractors, not the $6,000 paid to employees (ATO: Cleaning services). A pizza shop with $5,000 in delivery fees out of $100,000 (5%) doesn't lodge (ATO: Courier services).

Building and construction: the 50% test

The 10% test doesn't apply here. If your business is primarily in building and construction, you must lodge if you pay contractors or subcontractors for building and construction services and you have an ABN. The ATO says you are "primarily" in the industry if any of these apply (ATO: Building and construction services):

  • in the current financial year, 50% or more of your business income comes from building and construction services
  • in the current financial year, 50% or more of your business activity relates to building and construction services
  • in the financial year immediately before, 50% or more of your business income came from building and construction services.

For 2026-27, the "year before" is 2025-26. The ATO calls the definition of building and construction services broad. Its example trades include bricklaying, concreting, electrical and plumbing work, landscaping construction, painting and roofing.

What goes in a TPAR

For each contractor you pay, the ATO says your TPAR must include (ATO: TPAR contractor details to report):

Total GST you paid them
Total tax withheld where an ABN was not quoted

Report the totals in whole dollars, excluding cents. A few rules from the ATO's payments to report page catch people out:

  • Labour and materials on one invoice: report the total payment amount.
  • Cash basis: only report payments you made on or before 30 June. An invoice still unpaid at 30 June is left out.
  • Leave out: materials-only payments, incidental labour, labour-hire or on-hire workers, employees, payments within a consolidated group, and private and domestic projects.
  • No ABN quoted: payers must withhold 47% unless an exception applies, such as a payment of $75 or less excluding GST. Report the amount withheld in the TPAR or the NAT 3448 form, not both (ATO: Statement by a supplier not quoting an ABN).

Accuracy matters: the ATO says TPAR amounts will now be pre-filled in your contractors' tax returns (ATO: Taxable payments reporting and contractors).

How to lodge

TPARs are online only: the ATO says "TPAR paper lodgments are no longer accepted after 28 August 2025" (ATO: Lodge your TPAR). Your options:

  • business software, if it is SBR-enabled or can create a TPAR data file to the ATO's specification
  • Online services for business (you need an ABN, a Digital ID and Relationship Authorisation Manager)
  • Online services for individuals and sole traders
  • your registered tax or BAS agent.

Didn't pay contractors for services? The ATO says you should lodge a TPAR Non-lodgment advice instead. It is in ATO online services and can cover several years, including telling the ATO you won't need to lodge in future.
